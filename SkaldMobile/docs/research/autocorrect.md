# iOS system keyboard: autocorrection, spell check, prediction, learning (iOS 17–26)

Legend: **[D]** DOCUMENTED (Apple source) · **[O]** OBSERVED (credible hands-on/press/reverse-engineering) · **[I]** INFERRED. Thresholds Apple has never published are marked *unknown*.

## 0. Architecture in one paragraph

Two layers coexist. (a) A geometric/lexical corrector dating to the 2007 keyboard: every tap is a point, a typed word is a "constellation", candidates are dictionary words whose ideal constellations are close; score = usage frequency × 1/pattern-skew, highest wins [O, Kocienda via book notes]. Apple's patent US8232973 formalises this as touch-probability × usage-probability and adds invisible per-key hit-region resizing after every character [D]. (b) Since iOS 17, an on-device transformer LM re-ranks/predicts: a GPT-2-style, 6-block, 512-hidden, ~34 M-parameter model with a 15 k-token SentencePiece vocab (incl. 100 emoji and 79 contraction tokens) shipped in `RequiredAssets_en.bundle/…/unilm.bundle` [O, Jack Cook]; Apple says it "run[s] this model every time you tap a key" [D, WWDC23]. Language identification for multilingual input is a per-script character-level bi-LSTM (~4 MB total, Latin input capped at 10 chars) that selects "the right autocorrection lexicon and the right language model" [D, Apple ML Research 2019].

## 1. Trigger points and UI

- **When a correction is applied.** The candidate replaces the typed string when a delimiter is entered — space or punctuation ("touching the space bar or delimiter key triggers replacement … if that suggestion meets probability thresholds") [D, patent]; Return behaves as a delimiter too [O]. Tapping a QuickType candidate inserts it plus a trailing space [O]. Threshold values: *unknown*.
- **QuickType bar layout.** Three slots [O, TNW/AppleInsider]. Left: your literal string "shown as the option in quotation marks" [D, iPhone User Guide]; middle, highlighted: the autocorrection that Space will commit; right: an alternative [O, iPhoneLife]. If nothing is highlighted, Space keeps what you typed [O].
- **Backspace right after a correction.** Apple's documented recovery route: "Tap Delete, then tap the word you were in the process of typing" — deleting re-surfaces the original (quoted) in the bar [D, User Guide]. iOS does *not* SwiftKey-style auto-revert on a single backspace; it deletes the space/last character and re-offers the literal [O]. Deleting into a word and retyping it does not re-trigger the same correction "unless you drastically change the word" [O, TechRadar].
- **Temporary underline (iOS 17+).** "Auto-Correction temporarily underlines words that it corrects … tap the underlined word and choose an option from the pop-up menu" [D, Apple 104995]. The pop-up shows the original (marked with an arrow) plus other candidates; choosing the original teaches the dictionary ("won't automatically correct that word the next time") [O, MacStories]. Underline colour was reported blue (MacRumors) / yellow in dev beta 1 (Cult of Mac). How long "temporarily" lasts: *unknown* (disappears after further typing) [O]. Reliability: users reported the revert arrow and the ✕ failing in 17.1, fixed 17.2, regressing 17.3.1 [O, Apple Community]; repeated re-correction of a rejected word was documented by mjtsai [O].
- **Inline predictions (iOS 17+).** "Predictions that complete your word or phrase appear inline in gray text"; accept with Space, decline by continuing to type [D, User Guide]; multi-word completions appear only "where upcoming words are extremely obvious" [O, Cook]; "Multi-word suggestions are rare" [O, MacStories]. Requires iPhone 12 or later [D, Apple support/idB]. Languages: English (9 regions), French (4), Spanish (3) — nothing else, including Russian/Ukrainian [D, Apple feature-availability page]. Separate toggle "Show Predictions Inline" arrived in iOS 17.2 (Apple's support article says "iOS 18") [O, Wikipedia/idB vs D, 104995]; turning off "Predictive Text" kills both bar and inline predictions but keeps autocorrect [D].
- **When the bar is hidden.** Predictive Text off [D]; secure/password fields [O]; app sets `spellCheckingType = .no` — since iOS 15 `autocorrectionType = .no` alone no longer hides the bar (it shows spell-check candidates) [D, iOS 15 release note quoted in GitHub issue]; third-party keyboards replace it entirely [O]. Swipe-down-to-hide existed in iOS 8 only [O].
- **Grammar/sentence level (iOS 17+).** "Sentence-level autocorrections can fix more types of grammatical mistakes" [D, press release]; hands-on: affect/effect, then/than, your/you're flagged with a blue underline only "until you've finished each sentence" [O, MacRumors/Cult of Mac]; grammar DB existed in iOS 16 and grew in 17 [O, XDA]. A developer trait `grammarCheckingType` appears in docs only as iOS 27.0+ [D].

## 2. What gets corrected

- **Unknown words** → nearest lexicon word by combined touch + frequency score [D, patent; O, Kocienda]. Kocienda's team deliberately marked slurs so they are never *offered* [O].
- **Known→known contextual fixes** exist only where the transformer runs: English, Spanish, French per MacStories [O]; Apple published no explicit list — the inline-prediction language list is the best proxy [I]. Elsewhere the older per-word engine applies [I].
- **Profanity.** iOS 17 "can learn not to autocorrect swear words" (Federighi's "ducking" line) [D/O].
- **Capitalisation.** "Auto-Capitalization … capitalizes proper nouns and the first words in sentences as you type" [D, Apple support]; "i"→"I" is part of this [O]. Capitalisation of names is influenced by Contacts, including ALL-CAPS Exchange contacts leaking into suggestions [O, Apple Community].
- **Apostrophes/contractions.** The LM vocabulary carries 79 contraction tokens [O, Cook]; "dont"→"don't" normally fires, but community threads show inconsistency ("whats"→"what a") [O]. No documented rule.
- **Proper nouns.** Contact names (first/last/company) are treated as known words [O, Gadget Hacks / Apple Community]; Apple support does not document this.
- **Refusals.** No Apple document lists them. Widely observed: words typed in ALL CAPS, strings containing digits, "@", "/", ".", "#" (URLs, e-mails, hashtags) are left alone [I/O, unsourced]. Low confidence; treat as heuristics, not rules.
- **Near punctuation.** Punctuation is a delimiter, so `wrod.` corrects on the "."; "." shortcut (double-tap Space → ". ") only fires immediately after the word [D/O]. Smart Punctuation (quotes/dashes) is a separate substitution pass [D, `smartQuotesType`/`smartDashesType`].
- **Dynamic key target resizing.** Patent US8232973 (filed 2008, granted 2012): total hit area = visible + hidden; after each character the hidden area of likely next keys grows (after "Go" the D key expands because "God" is common); the visual keyboard never changes; the space key's target is enlarged when the word is probably complete [D]. It shipped in production iPhones ("Yup. It already is") [O, Gizmodo]. Magnitude: *unknown*; iOS 26.4's fix for "characters … appeared as if they were tapped, but were not actually inserted" shows the touch pipeline still gates autocorrect quality [D, release note via 9to5Mac]. Practically, it means iOS's corrector receives biased key hypotheses that an external app cannot reproduce from final text alone [I].

## 3. Learning

- **What enters the dynamic dictionary.** "You add words to the keyboard dictionary by rejecting words iPhone suggests as you type" [D, User Guide]. Typing an unknown word repeatedly makes iOS first leave it alone, then suggest it, then autocorrect *to* it [O, Macmost]; ✕/quoted-word rejection is the strongest signal [O]. Exact counts: *unknown* ("typed twice" is folklore).
- **Sources beyond typing.** Predictions use "your past conversations, writing style, and even websites you visit in Safari" [D, 104995]; iOS 8 press release: context includes "who the recipient is and in which app you're typing" [D, 2014]; Contacts names [O]. Since iOS 18.4 apps can hand the keyboard a `UIConversationContext` (mail/messaging thread) for suggestions [D].
- **How a learned word is used.** Suppresses correction, then appears as a candidate, then becomes a correction target [O].
- **Reset Keyboard Dictionary.** "erases only the words you've added" [D]; Text Replacements survive [O]. Reset All Settings also resets it [D].
- **Per-language.** Lexicons and LMs are loaded per detected language [D, Apple ML]; whether learned words are stored per language: *unknown* [I: likely per-language lexicon].
- **Text Replacement.** Highest priority: an expansion overrides autocorrect [O, Macmost]. To protect a word, add it with Phrase filled and Shortcut blank [D, User Guide]; syncs via iCloud Drive [D].
- **Differential privacy (opt-in via Device Analytics).** Features: "QuickType suggestions, Emoji suggestions, Lookup Hints…"; QuickType ε = 8, two donations/day; emoji ε = 4, one/day; Lookup Hints ε = 4, two/day; retention ≤ 3 months; visible under Settings › Privacy › Analytics Data as "DifferentialPrivacy.*" [D, Apple DP overview]. New out-of-vocabulary words are learned server-side via Sequence Fragment Puzzle and fed back to "on-device lexicons"; popular emoji are learned per locale [D, "Learning with Privacy at Scale"].
- **Personalisation model.** All personal learning is on-device [D]. Apple research on private federated personalised word-frequency histograms exists (NeurIPS 2024) [D]; press reports Apple used FL/DP for QuickType from iOS 13 [O, MIT Tech Review]; production status: *unknown*.

## 4. Multilingual

- **Language choice.** With several keyboards enabled, autocorrect follows the active keyboard's language; within the Multilingual Typing set, one keyboard accepts two languages and the bi-LSTM picks the lexicon per word [D/O]. iOS 10 introduced it [O, AppleInsider].
- **Multilingual Typing languages (Apple page, Sept 2026):** Bangla, Cantonese (Phonetic), Chinese Simplified/Traditional (Pinyin), Czech, Danish, Dutch (2), English (9), French (4), German (3), Gujarati, Hindi, Icelandic, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese (Romaji), Korean (2-Set), Lithuanian, Marathi, Norwegian (2), Polish, Portuguese (2), Punjabi, Romanian, Slovenian, Spanish (3), Swedish, Tamil, Telugu, Turkish, Vietnamese (Telex) [D]. **No Cyrillic language is listed.**
- **iOS 18.** One keyboard with English + up to two of Bangla/Gujarati/Hindi/Marathi/Punjabi/Tamil/Telugu, iPhone 12+ [D, Apple 121233]; MacStories observed Italian+English in one keyboard switching mid-sentence without red underlines [O].
- **iOS 26.** Arabizi transliteration keyboard; bilingual Arabic-English with real-time language detection; Hindi transliteration bilingual suggestions; "Natural Selection" for mixed LTR/RTL text [O, Gadget Hacks; D for `selectedRanges` API].
- **Russian and Ukrainian.** Both are in **Autocorrection** and **Predictive Typing** (bar) lists [D]. Neither is in **Inline Predictions**, **Multilingual Typing**, or **QuickPath** lists [D] (no swipe typing, no grey inline text, no cross-language autocorrect with English). Transformer/sentence-level and grammar corrections: not announced for either [I: absent]. Dictation supports both [D]. Consequence: a Russian keyboard corrects only against the Russian lexicon; Ukrainian words typed on a Russian layout (or vice versa) get treated as typos unless learned [I].

## 5. Fields and traits (UIKit; SwiftUI mirrors them)

- `autocorrectionType` (.default/.yes/.no): "If the script system doesn't support inline autocorrection, the keyboard input method ignores these constants" [D].
- `spellCheckingType`: red underlines + tap-to-fix; `.default` "enables spell-checking when autocorrection is also enabled"; **overrides the user's Settings › General › Keyboard choice** [D]. Setting `.no` hides the QuickType bar (iOS 15+) [D].
- `inlinePredictionType` (iOS 17+): `.default` decides "according to the context"; turn off if the app has its own suggestions [D].
- `mathExpressionCompletionType` (iOS 18+): "=" solves expressions in the bar [D/O]. `conversationContext` (iOS 18.4+) [D]. `writingToolsBehavior` (18.1+) [D]. `allowsNumberPadPopover` (26.1) [D]. `grammarCheckingType` (27.0+) [D].
- `smartQuotesType`/`smartDashesType`: region-specific glyphs / hyphen→en/em dash; `.default` "selectively enables … based on the keyboard type" [D]. User toggle: "Smart Punctuation" [O].
- `smartInsertDeleteType`: extra space after paste, remove "one or two spaces after a cut or delete" [D].
- `autocapitalizationType`: none/words/sentences/allCharacters; ignored for `numberPad`, `phonePad`, `namePhonePad` and unsupported scripts [D]. Sentence mode capitalises after ". ", "? ", "! ", at field start and after Return; after an opening quote/bracket at sentence start it still capitalises; after ":" or ";" it does not [I, no Apple doc].
- `textContentType`: lets the system "select an appropriate keyboard and improve keyboard corrections" [D]. Password (`isSecureTextEntry`, `.password`, `.oneTimeCode`): docs mention only copy/recording restrictions [D]; in practice autocorrect, predictions and the bar are off [O]. `.URL`, `.emailAddress`, `.webSearch` keyboards: autocap and autocorrect effectively off [O/I].
- "." shortcut: "Double-tap the Space bar" ends a sentence with period + space [D]; user toggle "." Shortcut [O]. Other user toggles: Auto-Capitalization, Auto-Correction, Check Spelling, Enable Caps Lock, Predictive Text, Show Predictions Inline, Slide to Type, Delete Slide-to-Type by Word, Character Preview, Smart Punctuation [O].

## 6. Other notable behaviour

- **Emoji.** "Suggested words, emoji, and information appear above the onscreen keyboard" [D]; in Messages, switching to the emoji keyboard turns replaceable words orange [D, Apple 102507]. Popular-emoji ranking per locale is DP-learned [D].
- **Undo.** Shake to Undo (toggle in Accessibility › Touch) [D]; three-finger swipe left / three-finger double-tap undo, swipe right redo (iOS 13+) [O]. Both undo the last text edit including a committed autocorrection [O].
- **QuickPath.** Swiped words get alternates in the bar; with "Delete Slide-to-Type by Word", one Delete removes the whole swiped word [D]. Transformer suggestions "do not work with swipe typing" [O, MacStories]; not available for Russian/Ukrainian [D].
- **Candidate counts.** Bar: 3 [O]. Underline pop-up: original + alternates, count varies [O].
- **Latency.** LM runs per keystroke [D]; typing is never blocked — corrections are resolved at the delimiter and, if the model lags, the literal is kept [I]. Lag/dropped taps were widely reported in 17.0 and 26.0–26.3; 26.4 "improves keyboard accuracy when typing quickly" [O/D].
- **Version deltas.** 17.0 transformer LM, underline+revert, inline predictions, sentence-level fixes; 17.2 separate inline toggle; 18.0 multilingual single keyboard, math completions; 18.4 conversation context; 26.0 Arabic/Hindi bilingual, Natural Selection; 26.4 tap-accuracy fix; 27 (out of scope) exposes `grammarCheckingType`.
